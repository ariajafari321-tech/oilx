# The 2026 Oil Shock

Anyone reading the recent data cold will think the files are broken. They aren't; 2026
has been a genuine supply shock, and it stress-tested every signal in the book.

What the raw files show (all verifiable in `data/raw`):
- **Brent at ~$125 vs WTI spot ~$96 in early October** — a Brent−WTI spread near $30,
  against a normal $3–6. Seaborne (Brent-priced) barrels carry a war premium that
  landlocked US crude doesn't.
- **Crack spreads exploded in late Q1**: RBOB roughly doubled Jan→Apr while crude rose
  ~50%, so the 3-2-1 crack went from ~$20 to $60+ per barrel.
- **US crude stocks built hard Feb→Apr** (420 → 466M bbl, a ~6-sd seasonal anomaly),
  then drew down just as hard May→July (to ~404M) before stabilizing ~424M.
- **OVX near 49** in October, double a calm year.

The reported cause: the **Iran conflict in early 2026 and the closure of the Strait of
Hormuz**, which cut crude and product flows, damaged regional refining, and pushed
diesel/jet margins to records (Dallas Fed: Brent 3-2-1 crack +176% from January by late
March; retail diesel +62% by April). Floating storage of sanctioned barrels reached ~300M
bbl by late February.

Why it matters for the strategy, signal by signal:
- **crack** read the margin spike as crude-bullish demand pull, but 2026's fat cracks
  were partly *refining-capacity damage* — the exact failure mode flagged in [[Signals]].
- **inv_level / inv_surprise**: the US *built* stocks during a global shortage (US crude
  was trapped and cheap relative to seaborne barrels), so the bearish inventory read
  fought the war rally. A US-centric indicator is a hemisphere-sized blind spot when the
  shock is on the water; a Brent-leg or freight-rate input is the obvious extension.
- **mom** caught the rally late but caught it; **cot** faded a crowd that kept being
  right for months — the dead zone and floor limited the damage.
- The **kill switch** fired once in the whole backtest — in this stretch — which is what
  it is for.
